01 / THE STARTING POINT

The problem behind the project.

Moving to an unfamiliar city creates several problems at once: finding somewhere to live, arranging food and learning where everyday services are. Those decisions are related, but information is usually scattered.

02 / PROJECT SCOPE

What this work explores.

C-STRAP stands for CrossState Student Residency & Amenities Platform. It is an academic/final-year project focused on the relocation experience. The portfolio records its problem and product scope; a public deployment and measured user outcomes are not available here.

03 / THE REASONING

Decisions worth explaining.

01

Organize around the student’s task

Accommodation and everyday essentials belong to one arrival journey. The product direction is to reduce the number of places a student has to search.

02

Trust is a product requirement

A useful next iteration needs listing provenance, freshness and a way to report inaccurate information. These are proposed validation priorities, not claims of existing verification features.

03

Validate the smallest useful experience

Before expanding the platform, test a narrow relocation journey with students and learn which decisions actually take the most time.

04 / EVIDENCE & NEXT STEP

Where the project stands.

Academic project overview. No customer counts, adoption metrics or production architecture are asserted. The next useful evidence would be a demonstrable user flow and structured feedback from relocation interviews.

What comes next

Develop a small product prototype and validate its discovery flow, information quality and prioritization with students who have recently moved.
